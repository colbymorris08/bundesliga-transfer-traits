# Independent Study Proposal / Memo

**Working title:** Which traits travel into the Bundesliga — and which source leagues should scouts prioritize?  
**Author:** Colby Morris · Berkeley Haas independent study  
**Scope:** Destination = **1. Bundesliga** only (inbound transfers into Germany’s top men’s division).  
**Public package:** https://github.com/colbymorris08/bundesliga-transfer-traits  

---

## What this project is

Clubs and scouts often ask two related questions when a player moves **into the Bundesliga** from another competition:

1. Which **performance traits** (passing volume, progressive carries, defensive actions, and so on) look **similar before and after** the move — i.e., which stats “travel” rather than reflecting only the prior league’s style or pace?  
2. Which **prior leagues** (and which prior trait profiles) line up with a stronger **first Bundesliga season**?

This project answers those questions with a fixed, transparent pipeline on public data. It is **not** a market-value or Transfermarkt study. The open-data success marker is **minutes played in the player’s first Bundesliga season** (“Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes”): more minutes means the club gave the arrival a real role.

The work extends a scouting-metric framework used in a New York Red Bulls (NYRB) project setting, written out as four stages rather than jargon arrows:

1. **Metric stability testing with a floor** — for each rate statistic, correlate the prior-league season with Year 1 in the Bundesliga; keep only metrics that clear a minimum correlation.  
2. **Metric redundancy testing with a floor** — among the surviving metrics, drop near-duplicates so the scout card is short and non-redundant.  
3. **Final per-position metric list → pizza / radar profiles** — use that shortlist to build player profiles (interactive explorer and pizza-style category radars).  
4. **League- and trait-based success testing** — ask which prior traits and feeder leagues line up with more Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes (and, exploratorily, whether league labels still matter after controls).

---

## Research question (explicit)

When players transfer **into the Bundesliga** from other leagues, which performance traits remain **stable across that league change**, and which **source (feeder) leagues** — and prior trait profiles — produce the most effective arrivals, where “effective” means **more Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes**?

“Stable vs. league-context” means: a high (or low) ranking on a per-90 rate in the prior league tends to recur in the first Bundesliga season, rather than disappearing once the player changes competition.

## Definitions (read once; used throughout)

| Term | Meaning in this memo |
|------|----------------------|
| **1. Bundesliga** | Germany’s top men’s professional league (destination for every analysis pair). |
| **Inbound / first Bundesliga season** | A player’s first season with recorded 1. Bundesliga minutes in the study window. |
| **Prior season** | The professional season immediately before that first Bundesliga season, played in a **non–1. Bundesliga** competition. |
| **Year 1 in Bundesliga** | That first Bundesliga season (sometimes abbreviated Y1 elsewhere; this memo spells it out). |
| **Feeder / source league** | The prior competition the player came from (e.g. Ligue 1, Championship, 2. Bundesliga). |
| **Per-90 / rate metric** | A counting or rate statistic scaled to 90 minutes played (volume and intensity comparable across minutes totals). |
| **Football Reference (FBref)** | Sports Reference’s public soccer tables — the **scale** layer (many leagues, season stats). |
| **StatsBomb Open Data** | Free event-level match data — the **deep** layer (fewer clean Bundesliga seasons, richer event metrics). |
| **Stability (Pearson r)** | Correlation of the **same** per-90 metric: prior season ↔ Year 1 in Bundesliga. High *r* ⇒ the trait travels. |
| **Redundancy (Pearson \|r\|)** | Correlation **between two different** metrics in the same season. Very high \|r\| ⇒ near-duplicates; keep one. |
| **Pizza / radar profile** | Category chart of the shortlisted traits (prior vs Year 1 in Bundesliga percentiles vs peers). |
| **Success (Phase 2)** | **Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes** (playing-time opportunity). Preferred club outcome would be Δ Transfermarkt market value; we do not scrape Transfermarkt (terms of service), so minutes are the open-data proxy. |
| **Spearman ρ** | Rank correlation used in Phase 2 (prior trait percentile ↔ Year 1 minutes). Robust when minutes are skewed. |
| **Analysis-ready pair** | One player with usable prior + Year 1 seasons that clear the **minutes floors** below — not every Bundesliga appearance. |

Inbound status is inferred from **playing records** (first Bundesliga season and prior competition ≠ 1. Bundesliga), not from scraped Transfermarkt transfer tables.

## Two evidence layers

| Tier | Role | Data | Analysis-ready N |
|------|------|------|------------------|
| **Tier 1 — Wide (FBref)** | Same pipeline at scale across many feeders | Football Reference player-season stats, **19 leagues** | **329** prior→Year 1 pairs |
| **Tier 2 — Deep (StatsBomb)** | Event-level check on the **same inbound logic** | StatsBomb Open Data, **17** competitions as priors | **96** cohort players |

FBref answers “which rates travel?” and “which feeders look strongest?” at honest multi-league sample size. StatsBomb asks whether **event-level trait families** also travel; it is **not** a second league-ranking study. StatsBomb priors mix club and international competitions (e.g. FIFA World Cup, UEFA European Championship, UEFA Champions League), so that layer is noisier by design.

**Attribution / use:** FBref / Sports Reference is viewable online and usable for coursework with attribution and rate-limited access — respect their terms; do not redistribute raw dumps beyond the course. StatsBomb Open Data is used where available under their open license.

## Design — who is in the sample?

### FBref — scale layer (N = 329)

- **Who:** Players with a first **1. Bundesliga** season in calendar end-years **2021–2025**.  
- **Minutes floors (both sides):** prior season ≥ **300′** and Year 1 in Bundesliga ≥ **300′**. Those floors — not “anyone who appeared in the Bundesliga” — produce **N = 329**.  
- **Prior sources:** season tables across **19 leagues** (Big Five plus feeders such as 2. Bundesliga, Eredivisie, Championship, Segunda, Serie B, and others listed below), with player-page fills when a league dump did not already cover the inbound.  
- **What we ran on rates:** **105 metrics tested** → **43 metrics met the stability floor** → **32 metrics met the redundancy floor** for the final scout shortlist.  
- **Why this design:** Honest multi-league N for “which traits travel?” and “which feeders look strongest on Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes?”

### StatsBomb — event deep dive (N = 96)

- **Who:** Same inbound logic on open data. Open 1. Bundesliga event dumps exist only for **2015/16** and **2023/24**. Priors come from **all male** open competitions/seasons in the working set (**17** competitions).  
- **Minutes floors (relaxed for thin open data):** prior ≥ **45′**, Year 1 in Bundesliga ≥ **30′**.  
- **Same pipeline order:**  
  1. **Stability** — correlate prior vs Year 1 in Bundesliga per-90. Gate **Pearson r ≥ 0.40** → **7 of 16** metrics pass.  
  2. **Redundancy** — near-duplicates only at **Pearson \|r\| ≥ 0.85** (so, for example, pass volume can stay alongside progressive passes) → **7 kept**.  
  3. **Pizza / explorer + success** — profiles from that shortlist; Phase 2 uses **Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes**.  
- **Role:** Confirm which trait *families* travel at event granularity — not to re-rank feeder leagues.

**FBref feeder dumps used (slimmed to inbound-roster players):** 2. Bundesliga · Eredivisie · Austria · Portugal · Belgium · Swiss · Turkey · Championship · Ligue 2 · Segunda · Serie B · Denmark · Poland · Czech (plus Big Five caches for Year 1 / Big-5 priors). Working analysis uses **19 leagues** in the FBref tables; paired inbound **N = 329**.

## Methodology (self-contained)

The core idea is **test–retest reliability across a league change**, not across two seasons in the same league. For each per-90 metric we compute **Pearson r** between the player’s prior-league season and their Year 1 in Bundesliga season.

### Stability floors (what “met the stability floor” means)

**FBref (category-specific):**

- Attacking / Passing / Other: **Pearson r ≥ 0.60**  
- Defending / Carrying: **Pearson r ≥ 0.50** (slightly lower so enough defensive and carrying metrics remain for a usable shortlist)

Result: **43 of 105** tested metrics cleared the floor.

**StatsBomb:** **Pearson r ≥ 0.40** → **7** metrics.

### Redundancy floors (what “met the redundancy floor” means)

Among metrics that already cleared stability, drop near-duplicates:

- **FBref:** **Pearson \|r\| ≥ 0.95** between two shortlist candidates. Pick order when choosing which to keep: higher stability first, then larger sample size, then prefer rate/contextual metrics → **32** remain.  
- **StatsBomb:** **Pearson \|r\| ≥ 0.85** → all **7** kept (including passes).

There are **no forced keeps** of vanity scoring stats outside that rule set.

### League-relative scaling (tested and not used)

We also tried scoring each rate as **“how high is this vs peers in the same prior league”** (within-league z-score or percentile) instead of the raw per-90. That **did not improve** prior→Year 1 in Bundesliga stability on FBref, so both sources keep **absolute per-90 rates**. On StatsBomb the idea is even less meaningful because priors mix World Cup / Euro / Champions League / clubs.

### Phase 2 — success indicators

After the shortlist exists:

- **Spearman** tests: prior-season trait **percentile** vs **Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes** (and secondary outcomes such as minutes percentile and a stable-trait profile score).  
- **Feeder summaries:** mean Year 1 outcomes by prior league (descriptive).  
- **Exploratory OLS** on a Big-5 FBref subset (**N = 117**): Year 1 minutes ~ prior league (Serie A as baseline) + prior minutes + position, then models that add prior trait percentiles. Associative only — not a validated forecast. Headline pattern: **league gaps look modest**; adding traits raises fit more than league labels alone (see README / deck for coefficients).

## Steps (how the work was done)

1. Build the inbound cohort: first 1. Bundesliga season in the study window, prior season ≠ 1. Bundesliga, **and** clear the minutes floors (FBref prior / Year 1 ≥ **300′**; StatsBomb prior ≥ **45′** / Year 1 ≥ **30′**); pull that prior-league season’s stats.  
2. **Stability** — correlate prior → Year 1 in Bundesliga; apply the source-specific Pearson floors above.  
3. **Redundancy** — drop near-duplicates with the pick rules above; shortlist the scout card (**32** FBref · **7** StatsBomb).  
4. **Profiles & significance** — explorer / pizza graphics; Spearman trait and league tests vs **Year 1 in Bundesliga minutes**; exploratory OLS on the Big-5 subset.

## Why this design works for a one-credit / independent-study package

- **Honest N:** FBref **329** paired seasons for the scale story; StatsBomb **96** for event depth — not a claim that open data alone has hundreds of clean feeder→Bundesliga event pairs.  
- **Narrow destination:** only into the Bundesliga, so “travel” has one clear meaning.  
- **Clear pipeline:** stability → thresholds → redundancy → explorer → significance, each defined above.  
- **Multi-league FBref:** same method beyond Big-Five-only anecdotes.  
- **Accurate roles for each source:** FBref for portable rates and feeder readouts; StatsBomb as a smaller, deeper event check.  
- **Self-contained package:** memo + deck + HTML tools; regenerable analysis tables stay local (`results/`, gitignored) while headline numbers live in this memo, the README, and the deck.
